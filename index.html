01

GERMANY / BEFORE THE CHARTS

Before the audience, an everyday life.

Before MMCrypto, Christopher Jaszczyński was an economics student in Germany, balancing his studies with work as a taxi driver. His days belonged to classrooms and the road, well before daily market analysis became his routine.

That is where this story begins: with a working student, an interest in economics, and a life that had yet to turn toward the camera. The creator people know today came later.

02

SEPTEMBER 2017 / THE FIRST TURN

A discovery becomes a new direction.

In 2017, Christopher discovered crypto and began a change that would reshape his working life. He left his studies and taxi work to pursue investing and content creation, moving toward a future that was still taking shape.

His YouTube channel launched in September 2017. What started as a new interest now had a public home: a place to study markets, share analysis and begin building a community.

THE EARLY DAYS / THE QUIET WORK

Showing up before the breakthrough.

During the first year, posting brought little or no income. Christopher kept creating, supported by the savings he had earned driving taxis. The work was already happening, even while the financial return from it remained uncertain.

That period gives the story its human scale: an early creator continuing to publish, learn and explain. Before the platform grew, there was the recurring task of making the next piece of content.

THE CHANNEL / A SHARED PRACTICE

One voice. An ongoing conversation.

Daily charts and market analysis became central to MMCrypto. Christopher built his content around returning to the markets, sharing his reading of them and keeping a conversation open with the people following along.

Over time, the channel grew into a community around that ongoing practice. The story expanded beyond a creator and his uploads to include the audience gathering around the analysis, the questions and the next market move.

WEBSITE EDITORIAL / PUBLIC NOTE

Read a market view with context

Editorial learning material prepared for this website. Not authored by Chris.

A market commentary is one person's interpretation of incomplete information. Reading it carefully means understanding the argument, checking its evidence, and noticing what remains uncertain.

01 Identify the actual claim

Separate observations from interpretations and predictions. “This happened” and “this may happen next” require different kinds of evidence. If the central claim is unclear, summarize it in one plain sentence.

02 Check the source and timing

Look for original sources, publication dates, and the period being discussed. A recent article can rely on older information. A chart or quotation may be accurate while leaving out context that changes its meaning.

03 Look for missing alternatives

Ask what other explanation could fit the same facts. Notice assumptions about timing, market conditions, or participant behavior. A useful argument makes its limitations visible.

04 Keep confidence separate from evidence

An assured tone, repeated claim, or large audience does not establish reliability. Consider whether the author discloses relevant interests and explains what evidence would change their view.

TRY THIS / A READING CARD

After reading a commentary, write down its main claim, strongest supporting source, one assumption, and one unanswered question. If you cannot locate supporting evidence, mark that gap before drawing a conclusion.

01 Understand the mechanics

Be able to explain what you would hold, how the order works, and which costs or restrictions apply. Check settlement, liquidity, and any borrowing exposure. Protective orders may execute differently from what you expect.

02 Make risk explicit

Consider the possible loss alongside existing commitments and emergency needs. If borrowing or leverage is involved, understand whether losses could exceed the initial amount committed. An unclear downside is a reason to pause and investigate.

03 Check your decision conditions

Notice urgency, fatigue, frustration, or a desire to recover a previous loss. Ask whether the idea still makes sense without social pressure or a deadline created by someone else.

04 Plan a useful review

Record your reasoning, assumptions, and unresolved questions before acting. Later, assess whether you followed a considered process. A favorable outcome alone does not establish that the reasoning was sound.

BEFORE ACTING / FIVE QUESTIONS

  • Can I explain the mechanics, costs and restrictions?
  • Have I considered the downside and my financial commitments?
  • Can I describe my reasoning without relying on someone's confidence?
  • Have I recorded uncertainties and questions?
  • Am I willing to pause or take no action?

For learning and reflection. This note is not a recommendation to enter a trade.
